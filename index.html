Where these rates come from

S&P 500 with dividends reinvested, 1928–2025: 10.02% a year compounded (11.85% simple average), about 6.8% a year after inflation; inflation averaged about 3.0% a year. Source: Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern, “Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills: 1928–2025” (updated January 2026), pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar.

Large US stocks, 1926–2025: 10.07% a year compounded; inflation 2.94% a year. Source: Ibbotson equity indices (CRSP and US Bureau of Labor Statistics data) in CFA Institute Research Foundation, “Exponential Wealth” (2026).

The presets sit at or below that history on purpose: 10% optimistic (the long-run average), 8% base and 6% conservative, because future returns may be lower and real investors pay fees and miss some days. 3% inflation matches the long-run average.

Math: monthly compounding, with the monthly rate set so 12 months equal the yearly rate; contributions go in at the end of each month. Real returns vary a lot year to year; a smooth line is an average path, not a forecast.
